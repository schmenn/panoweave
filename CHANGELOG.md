# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- Near objects count less when aligning: a car 3 m in front of the camera (or a railing, a passer-by) shifts between the shots in a way a camera that only turns cannot explain, and points and patches on it pulled the whole alignment away from the background. The program now finds the near objects per photo pair - from the matches the rotation model rejects (second RANSAC of the parallax hint, which now also reports the cells of the group in both photos) and, after every alignment and every fine-alignment round, from the points and patches whose remaining error is large (at least 8 px at 1600 px), points the same way (similar error vector), lie together in the picture (one connected region) and leave enough points that fit (a group of at most 60 % of the pair; a pair that is off as a whole - wrong lens, wrong start - is not taken for an object) - as coarse masks (16 x 16 cells, one ring added) in both photos. A point or patch whose two ends both lie in such a mask gets a quarter of its weight (`ControlPoint::depth_w`, combined with the foliage factor in `ControlPoint::opt_weight`, which the optimiser uses; the step acceptance uses it too, otherwise steps that ignore the object would be refused). Protected points and points placed by hand are never touched. Switch "Count points and patches on near objects less" in step 3 (on by default; off = every factor 1 and nothing searched, exactly as before), settings in `depth::DepthConfig` (also `FineConfig::depth`; `PW_DEPTH=0`, `PW_DEPTH_W=<factor>`), the status line after aligning names the photo pairs. `--accuracy` prints the near objects found and a second line `BACKGROUND` with the check spots off near objects. Unit-tested on synthetic pairs (background plus a shifted near object: the object is found, its points are down-weighted, the background wins - rotation error under 0.6 of the unweighted one; without an object nothing changes; switched off; protected and hand-placed points; a pair that is off as a whole; mask cells and pair orientation). Measured with `--accuracy` (3 passes, median / mean / worst 10 % of the check spots): street series with a car close to the camera (19 photos, project) 1.347 / 1.417 / 2.533 px (rating off) against 1.327 / 1.390 / 2.462 px (on) in pass 1, 1.311 / 1.408 / 2.567 against 1.311 / 1.386 / 2.486 in pass 2, so the background is a little better, within the run-to-run noise of this series; the 13 test photos IMG_7180-7192 0.271 / 0.308 px both ways. Limits of 4 px and 6 points found near objects in almost every pair of the street series (the parallax of trees) and made pass 2 worse (median 1.55), so the defaults are 8 px and 8 points; with them only a few pairs are reported, so the effect on this series is small.
- Hint at nearby objects (parallax): after the normal RANSAC of a photo pair, the matches it rejected get a second RANSAC of their own. If a large group of them agrees among itself (own homography), lies in one connected region of the picture and sits clearly away from where the rotation model puts it, the pair is reported: "Photos 8 and 10: 412 matches on a nearer object do not fit the rest (about 25 px apart) - this looks like parallax." in the status line after "Find points", in step 3 (card "Nearby object" with the buttons "Turn on Parallax mode" and "Open masks", only on click) and in the connection view. The matches of that group are only counted, never turned into points (the rotation model cannot fulfil them), so the points, the alignment and `--accuracy` stay exactly as before. Every finding and the number of checked pairs are written to the log (`parallax check:`). Switch "Hint at nearby objects" next to the point finder (on; off = no extra work; also off while Parallax mode is on), limits in `features::ParallaxHintConfig` (`min_count` 40, `min_share` 0.10 of the pair's matches, `min_shift_px` 8 at 1600 px, `min_coherence` 0.7). Unit-tested: synthetic pair with a nearer object (reported with its size, shift and place, same points as without the check), without it (no hint), random wrong matches (no hint), scattered consistent matches (no hint), switched off.
- Optional "Maximum accuracy" in step 3 ("Try all ways", off unless pressed, may take long): places the photos in every way the program has - all together (as "Align photos"), a frame from the protected points first and the other photos attached to it, and photo by photo from several start photos (strongest link first, or outward in shooting order; every new photo is placed from its best link and then all photos placed so far are optimised together again, so a later photo can pull an earlier crooked one right) - each with and without the fine alignment, and rates every way with the independent check on the same fixed spots. The ranking uses only the spots every finished way could measure; a way that loses spots others could measure shows it in "Lost". The table says which way was how good and which was left out (time budget, no protected points); the best one is only proposed: "Apply best" / "Apply" on a row, or "Keep current". Settings: start photos (3), time budget (30 min), with or without fine alignment. Headless: `--accuracy-search <photos or project.pwproj>` prints the table (`PW_ACCS_STARTS`, `PW_ACCS_BUDGET`, `PW_ACCS_FINE=0`, `PW_ACCS_WEIGHTS=1,30`, `PW_ACCS_CHAINS=0`, `PW_ACCS_CHECK_SIZE`). Parallax mode is not scored (the check measures the rotation model only); the foliage filter is a hook until that feature exists. Unit-tested on synthetic scenes (every way rated on the same spots, a protected weight beats weight 1, no frame way without protected points, time budget, cancel, current alignment as a way, identical results measured once).
- `--accuracy <project.pwproj>` uses the photos, lenses and points of a project (protected points included); prints the fit of the protected points per pair (also what two photos alone could reach with only those points), the overlapping pairs that still have too few points, and takes `PW_ACC_LOCKED_WEIGHT`.
- Foliage: points and patches on trees and bushes get a smaller weight when aligning (down to a quarter) instead of being kept at full strength or deleted. Foliage is recognised by colour (green to brown), very fine irregular structure (a map per photo on the working image) and, above all, by restless remaining errors after aligning (a point whose error and whose neighbours' errors disagree); colour and structure alone only lower the weight a little, so a green sign, a green wall or a lawn that holds still keep their influence. The fine alignment no longer prefers foliage for its control areas. Protected points and points placed by hand are never touched. Switch "Count points and patches on foliage less" in step 3 (on by default; off = exactly as before), settings in `FoliageConfig` (also `FineConfig::foliage`, `FoliageConfig::enabled(bool)` for runs that decide per run, `PW_FOLIAGE=0|1`). New in the point editor: **Foliage → Remove N** removes the points on foliage on request (one undo step, hand-placed and protected points stay). `--accuracy` prints the share of points and patches on foliage and can write overlay pictures (`PW_FOLIAGE_OVERLAY`). Unit-tested with synthetic leaves, a smooth green sign and moving versus still errors.
- Point editor: new **Area** tool. Draw a rough rectangle round the same surface (a sign, a number plate, a wall) in the left and in the right photo, in either order. The drag starts where the mouse button went down (not a few pixels later, where egui reports it). Afterwards the four corners are dots that can be pulled one by one, so a surface seen at an angle (the number plate of a car standing sideways) can be outlined; dragging inside moves the whole area, dragging outside draws it again, a change that would make the sides cross is undone, Esc clears. "Match the areas" compares the two areas on a worker thread: the start guess is the projective map (homography) of the four corners onto the four corners (plus shift/zoom and the rotation of nearby points or of the alignment as further guesses); spots are corners: places with strong structure over the whole patch (smaller eigenvalue of the structure tensor, no plain surface, no single straight edge) that are local maxima of the corner strength on a small scale, moved onto the vertex of the edges round them (sub-pixel), so points sit on the corners of a sign and not on the smooth surface beside them; every spot is matched with the patch matcher (ZNCC, pyramid, sub-pixel) through the local linearisation of the current homography, the homography (8 parameters, affine with fewer than 8 matches, only a shift with fewer than 4; a fit that tears the area apart is refused) is fitted again from the matches (robust, three rounds), so corners drawn a bit off (`corner_tolerance`, default 15 % of the area) are corrected automatically, and misfits are dropped. After matching the corrected right area (the left one carried over by the fitted map) can be shown dashed ("Show corrected area", `show_corrected`). The proposed points are shown with their scores (weak ones marked and not ticked) and are only added after "Accept" as hand-placed points, in one undo step; "Discard" drops them. A plain area, areas that do not show the same thing, a tiny or crossed area give a clear message. Switch and settings in `AreaConfig` (`enabled`, `max_points`, `min_texture`, `corner_rel`, `corner_tolerance`, `min_zncc`, `weak_zncc`, ...; `PW_AREA=0` switches the tool off); `--demo area`. Unit-tested on synthetic photos (shifted, zoomed and turned areas, and a perspective-slanted area with corners drawn up to 18 px off on a 180 x 120 px area: points within 0.5 px of the truth and the corrected corners within 3 px; a bright bar whose proposed points lie within 0.5 px of its corners in both photos; homography fit with and without a wrong match); not tried by clicking through the window on real photos.

### Changed
- Point editor, add tool: the side decides. You click in one photo (the start side), the program searches the partner in the other. A further click in the photo where it searched corrects that proposal (local snap around the click, Alt = exact, as often as wanted and also far from the wrong proposal); a click in the start side begins a new point, so Esc or Ctrl+click are no longer needed (both still work). Mirrored when you start on the right. The start side of the new point is remembered in `pe.fresh`; protected points are still not moved. Hint line, status messages and the user guide are updated. Unit-tested; not tried by clicking through the window.

### Fixed
- Alignment ignored protected points: they counted like any single automatic point, so a few hand-placed, protected points lost against hundreds of automatic points and control areas, some of them wrong (parallax on a car, trees, smooth surfaces). On a real project (19 photos, 40 protected points) the protected points fitted to 31 px on average (largest 71 px, original photo pixels) after aligning; now 7.4 px (largest 21 px) with the default weight 5, 4.4 px (largest 16 px) with 30. Protected points now count `LOCKED_WEIGHT` (5) times as much (a compromise: on that project, where many protected points sit on a near car, 30 fitted them best but left about a fifth fewer spots of the rest of the picture measurable), stay quadratic over a wider range (they are not down-weighted as outliers) and a pair with at least 3 protected points takes only those for its start position. Switch: "Weight of protected points" in step 3 (1 = as before). After aligning, the status line says how well the protected points fit.
- Pair check and connection view counted control areas (up to 200 per pair, measured along the seams) as points, so a pair held together only by areas looked well connected, was never searched again and showed green. Only points count now (a hand-drawn area still does). Unit-tested; the case of three photos in a stack with points only A-B and B-C already worked (A-C was found through the geometry and got 20 points, unit-tested).
- The optimiser accepted a step by the plain sum of squares although it minimises the robust loss, so steps that ignore wrong points were refused and the robust weighting could not do its job. It now accepts by the robust loss it minimises.
- Point finder and pair check: `features::thin` went through its 8x8 grid row by row from the top and stopped at the limit (20 per pair), so the points sat almost only in the upper part of the picture and objects further down (a car, a number plate) got none, even without parallax. It now takes the best match of each cell round by round, visiting the cells in a scattered order, so a limit below the cell count still covers the whole overlap. `MatchSettings::even_thinning = false` (`PW_THIN_OLD=1` in `--accuracy`/`--selftest`) restores the old order. Unit-tested.
- Fine alignment: a protected point was reported as "could not be confirmed (no match nearby - maybe far off)" when the search was ambiguous (repeating pattern, number plate, sign) or ended at the search edge, although the point was right. It is now confirmed at its own spot (similarity there at least the minimum score); the search's uniqueness test no longer counts. The message gives the reasons with a count (repeating pattern, too close to the photo edge, too little texture, too little similarity). Protected points are still never moved. Unit-tested.
- "N bad points removed automatically" added up over the alignment passes of the pair check and counted control areas, which the fine alignment rebuilds anyway. The clean-up now leaves automatic control areas out (`AlignSettings::outliers_skip_areas`, `PW_OUTLIER_AREAS=1` in `--accuracy` for the old way) and the message reads "N points removed in M passes". Unit-tested.
- Fine alignment: control areas no longer sit on smooth asphalt or line up in rows on trees. The check for texture only looked at the amount of gradient, so a single lane edge or faint asphalt grain passed, and the areas were put on a bare grid wherever it fell. Now a patch must have structure in two directions (smaller eigenvalue of the structure tensor, `MatchConfig::min_structure` = 1e-4; a straight edge is refused with the new reason "structure in one direction only"), and every area is placed at the spot with the strongest structure within half a grid step of its grid point (`FineConfig::pick_textured`, on); grid cells without any are left out and counted ("N grid cells without structure were left out"). Settings: "Where the photo has structure" in the fine alignment settings; `min_structure = 0` and `pick_textured = false` give the earlier behaviour. Measured on a street series (19 photos, all rounds of two alignment passes): 12.9 % of the accepted patches had a structure below 1e-4 and every accepted patch lay on the bare grid; now none is below, and 88 % instead of 67 % of the tried spots are accepted. The independent fit (`--accuracy`) is unchanged within its run-to-run variation (`--accuracy`, 2 passes, median / mean shift of the check spots: street series 1.231 / 1.336 px before, 1.229 / 1.310 px after; the 13 test photos IMG_7180-7192 0.270 / 0.309 px before, 0.275 / 0.308 px after (p90 0.559 -> 0.543); the fine alignment itself hardly changes the fit on either set, and the street series is limited by the parallax of near trees and cars. The check itself keeps the earlier matcher settings so it measures the same in both runs).
- Fine alignment: automatic control areas stay half the patch plus half the search radius (was: the whole search radius) from the photo border (`FineConfig::edge_search_share` = 0.5, 1.0 = earlier behaviour), so more of them reach the edges where the lens distortion shows. A cut-off at about 20 % from the border, as reported, was not found: automatic points and areas of the street series reach every distance from the border up to the middle of the photo (1680 points: 40 / 254 / 449 / 447 / 297 / 193 in the bands 0-2 / 2-5 / 5-10 / 10-15 / 15-20 / 20-23 % of the long side; the photos are 1600 x 738 px, so 23 % is the middle). What was found is the fixed distance of areas from the border (3.7 %, now 2.75 %).
- Point finder: detected points sat beside the corner they belong to. The peak of the blurred corner response lies inside the corner (0.9 px at full size on a test rectangle, up to 3.5 px on the coarser pyramid levels); every point is now moved onto the vertex of the edges around it (least-squares intersection of the edge lines; `PW_CORNER_SNAP=0` switches it off): 0.12 px at full size, at most 0.6 px on the coarse levels. The conversion from a pyramid level back to full size used the nominal scale instead of the real size ratio of the rounded level, which moved points by up to a pixel far from the origin. Unit-tested on a rectangle with known corners.
- Point list: protected points could not be unlocked there, so they could not be deleted from it. Each row has a lock to protect or unlock it (all selected rows when it is part of the selection), and "Unlock selected", "Unlock all listed" and "Protect selected" act on several; one undo step, status message. Editor key L and the list share one function. Unit-tested; not tried by clicking through the window.
- "Find more points for these two photos" replaced all automatic points of the pair, also the ones the person had checked. It now only adds: existing points stay, a found point closer than 0.004 (normalised, about 6 px) to a point of the pair in either photo is skipped, and the message says "N new, M already there". Points moved by "Snap selected" now count as placed by hand (dragging already did). Unit-tested.
- Point editor, add tool: a wrongly snapped side could not be corrected (every click went to the same spot, and clicking the first photo again made a second point). While the new point is still selected, a click in a photo moves exactly that side there; the snap only corrects within the small local search radius and never jumps back, a weak match keeps the click spot, Alt+click and Alt+drag do not snap at all, protected points are not moved. Esc or Ctrl+click starts a new point (hint line and status explain it). Unit-tested; not tried by clicking through the window.
- Point editor: both photos are equal. It does not matter whether you start in the left or the right photo, a correction click, Alt, snapping and drag act on the clicked side only, and the match window of a new point follows the half placed second. The local tilt for a correction no longer counts the point being corrected. Unit-tested with both photos on either side.

### Changed
- One lasso in the whole program. The point editor and the mask editor now share one implementation (`select_shapes::Lasso`) and one way of drawing it. The point editor's lasso gains what the mask lasso had: besides dragging around an area, you can click corner by corner and close with a double click or Enter; Esc cancels it, Ctrl+Z takes the last corner back, a shaky click sets one corner. Both editors draw the same outline (dark halo, thin line, faint closing line, corner dots, brush circle with the same halo) and show the same help text; only the colour differs (violet for points, green/red for the kind of mask). With the lasso chosen in the point editor, a click on empty space now starts a corner instead of clearing the selection; a click on a point still selects it. Unit-tested; not tried by clicking through the window.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-11-09

### Added
- `--accuracy` measures on 3200 px copies of the photos by default (was the 1600 px working images, where part of the result was the inaccuracy of the measurement itself: 0.40 instead of 0.27 px on the Fischerbastei set). `PW_ACC_CHECK_SIZE` still sets it.
- Headless `--accuracy <photos>`: aligns like the Align button and measures the remaining shift in every overlap on spots that are neither points nor control areas (`fine_align::quality`), so removing points cannot improve it; per pass, by distance from the photo centre and optionally per pair (`PW_ACC_PAIRS=1`). Documented in the README, the guide and `CLAUDE.md`; unit test with a known misalignment. Measured with it (Fischerbastei 13 photos, Citadelle 21 photos): aligning again lowers the shown average error (0.60 → 0.54 px) but not the real fit; fine alignment on 3200 px images, a second distortion term and accepting optimiser steps by the robust cost (review C4) changed the real fit by 0–3 %, so no "maximum accuracy" mode was added.
- User guide (`docs/GUIDE.md`): quick start, how to take photos that stitch well, every step and its settings, the point editor, the extra windows, exposure blend, projects, pitfalls with solutions, what the messages mean, keys and mouse. With screenshots; linked from the README and the help window.

### Fixed
- Masks: a spot masked red ("do not use") still got into the panorama. Where every photo covering it was masked, one of them was used anyway (a boat masked in every photo stayed), and elsewhere the blending mixed in the coarse part of the masked photo, so a removed boat looked blurred instead of gone. Now a red spot does not exist for its photo: nothing of it is blended in (Smooth seams and Soft fade, preview, brightness matching and the smooth brightness correction), another photo fills it, and where none is left the panorama has a hole (transparent in PNG and TIFF, black in JPEG). The preview and "Frame & crop" show it, a warning in "Frame & crop", the export step and the message after the export says how big it is; "Auto crop" ignores mask holes. Masks now also refresh the preview. Unit-tested (nothing of a red photo blended in, hole where only it covers, `mask_holes`, the strip height still changes nothing).
- Masks: the lasso only took corners clicked one by one; a click that moved a few pixels was taken as a drag and lost, and dragging around an area (as with the lasso of the point editor) did nothing. Now dragging around an area masks it on release, clicking corners still works, and a shaky click still sets its corner. Unit-tested; not tried by clicking through the window.
- Pair check: points found again for a weak pair that the pair already had (same spot in both photos, 2 px tolerance as for duplicate points) were counted as new, could make the pair look solved and then counted twice in the alignment. They are now left out and named in the step's note and the log ("n already there"). Measured: none on the Fischerbastei set, 1–4 of about 20 in 7 search steps on the Citadelle set; the real fit (`--accuracy`) is the same (median 0.533 → 0.534 px). Unit-tested.
- The window no longer stands still while brightness matching is worked out (after aligning, opening a project, letting go of the view in step 4, and the two brightness switches in the export step). Measured before: 50–160 ms without and 450–700 ms with the lens correction (13 and 21 photos). It now runs on a worker thread; the preview keeps the old values until the new ones arrive, "Export panorama" waits for them (greyed out meanwhile), a result made for photos that were added or removed meanwhile is dropped, and a worker that fails is reported. Export pixel-identical to before (with and without lens correction and gain maps, `--imgdiff`). Unit-tested; not tried by clicking through the GUI.
- Patch comparison window: a patch shifted by hand while the fine alignment was still running was overwritten when the round ended (it could even turn "rejected"); now the hand shift and its status stay and only the automatic result is updated. After a restart with patches shifted by hand the live preview stopped for good (the live focus was never given back); it keeps running now. A patch shifted outside photo B showed as valid although it could not be used; it is now rejected with the reason "shifted by hand outside photo B". Sorting the list by a column with a missing score (after a shift by hand) could crash the program; it sorts with a total order now. Unit-tested; not tried by clicking through the window.
- Fine alignment: the two fine sub-pixel steps (0.25 and 0.1 px) applied their correction twice, so they only mirrored the remaining error instead of making it smaller. On a test picture with known shifts the mean error drops from 0.025 px to 0.002–0.005 px (largest 0.068 → 0.015 px); a unit test checks it. On real photos (Fischerbastei 13 photos, Citadelle 21 photos, each run twice) the difference is within the run-to-run variation: same point errors, same time, 2 and 5 more valid patches, Citadelle's mean pixel difference in the overlaps 11.5 → 11.25.
- TIFF export: the fourth channel is now marked as transparency (`ExtraSamples` = unassociated alpha); other programs may have shown the area outside the panorama black or as an unnamed extra channel.
- JPEG/TIFF export: the end of the file is written and checked before the export reports success (before, an error at the very end, e.g. a full disk, was lost and a cut-off file kept). When the JPEG encoder fails, the message names the real cause (e.g. disk full) instead of "the JPEG encoder stopped unexpectedly".
- Export: a typed file name whose extension does not fit the file type (e.g. "pano.png" with JPEG chosen) gets the right extension, and the dialog says so before the export; a JPEG is no longer written into a ".png" file. The dialog also says when an existing file will be replaced. Blended exposures: an unknown extension becomes ".jpg".
- Opening a project whose photos are partly missing kept nothing of the project (points, alignment, masks were dropped, the warning was overwritten at once by "Project opened"). Now the photos that are there keep their points, alignment and masks; the message names the missing files and how many points to them were left out, and the next save asks for a file name instead of offering the old one.
- Cancelling while a project loads no longer applies that project to the photos added next.
- Removing a photo, "New" or opening another project no longer crashes the connections window (it still pointed at the old photo number); the patch click, pair report, overlap picture and list caches are dropped with it. The clicks of the patch click mode are dropped when another photo pair is shown, and a result that arrives for a pair no longer on screen is not used.
- "New"/opening a project resets the export file name and the last exported file (an export of the new project went into the old project's folder), "align again", and drops a preview still being computed for the old project.
- Project files: opening checks that lens and photo numbers exist and that the file is not from a newer version (a damaged file is reported instead of crashing later). Saving writes a temporary file first and replaces the old one only when complete; a value that is not a number in the geometry stops the save with a message instead of writing a file that cannot be opened again, invalid measurement values of points are saved as 0 and counted in the message.
- Fine alignment: protected points are no longer moved, re-weighted or dropped (before, a protected point that was not placed by hand got a new position in photo B, and with "drop invalid points" it could be removed). They are still measured: when the spot found in photo B is more than `locked_warn_px` (3 px of the working image) from the point, or no match is found nearby, the status line and the fine-alignment summary say how many and how far, so the person can check them ("only protected" in the point list); the note is also added to the "unconnected image groups" warning so it is not overwritten. A protected control area is kept too.
- Fine alignment with "Add control areas" switched off measured nothing to judge the rounds with, stopped at once and wrote "remaining shift below the threshold – done". It now says that nothing was changed and why.
- A job that failed with an internal error (a panic in the worker) left the program "busy" for ever with no message. Now the status line says which job failed and why, names the crash log, and nothing is changed; the next job works. A preview or blended preview whose worker ended without a picture no longer blocks every later preview.
- The locks of the patch store, the export photo cache, the sky cache of the pair check and the JPEG writer keep working after a panic in another thread (before, one failure made every later access fail too).
- The "use" tick in the photo list is locked while a job runs (an alignment that finished after a tick changed could duplicate or lose points).

### Added

- Points on sky or cloud: new switch "Find points on sky or cloud" (off by default; in the point editor next to "Clean up" and in the list of all points). The sky mask of the photos is made on a worker thread (button "Check N photos", cancelable, kept in memory only, not saved in the project). Every point is checked in both photos with the same cloud score the point finder uses; it counts when the share of sky around it is above the threshold (0.3 to 0.8, default 0.5) in either photo. The list marks these rows blue and has the filter "only those"; "Remove N" removes them as one undo step. Protected points are only marked and never removed; points placed by hand only with "include points placed by hand" (off by default). When nothing can be removed the button says why ("0 to remove (2 by hand, 1 protected)"), and the status line says how many stayed. After the check the toolbar says "sky checked in N photos" (the masks stay in memory, new points are checked at once). Control areas are not judged. Switched off, nothing is computed and nothing is shown. Tried in the GUI by the contributor (before the hand-placed option).
- Point editor: "Snap selected (n)" refines every selected point with the same correlation search as after dragging a point (local zoom/tilt from the other points of the pair, sub-pixel), as a background job with progress and Cancel (nothing is changed when cancelled). The right photo's side of the pair on screen is searched, the left one stays; a result is only taken when the match is at least 80 %. Hand-placed points are snapped too, protected points never. Status line: "n snapped, m unchanged (match too low), k protected skipped". One undo step. If the points change while the job runs, the result is dropped (all) or skipped (single points that were edited meanwhile). Unit-tested (displaced point snaps back, flat area stays, cancel, protected, stale results, one undo step); not tried by clicking through the GUI.
- Point editor: selection shapes as a choice inside "Select / edit" (Click, Rectangle, Lasso, Brush with radius), shown only while "Select / edit" is on. Rectangle and lasso are dragged on empty space (a point under the mouse is still dragged as usual); the brush selects on a single click already and while painting. Plain drag replaces the selection with the points inside; Shift adds; Ctrl takes points out (with the brush, painting adds and Ctrl subtracts). The shape acts on the points of the photo half it was dragged in, for the pair on screen; the right mouse button still moves the photo. Tried in the GUI by the contributor (before the layout change).
- Point editor: multiple selection. Shift or Ctrl+click on a point in either photo adds it to the selection or takes it out again (extra ring around selected points); in the point list (F3) Ctrl+click toggles a row and Shift+click selects the range up to it. "Delete selected (n)" (button and Delete key) removes the whole selection as one undo step. Protected points are never deleted; the message names them. The selection is dropped when the points change in a way that moves their numbers (undo, find, clean up, other photo pair), so it never points at other points. Without Shift/Ctrl everything works as before. Unit-tested; not tried by clicking through the GUI.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-11-05

First public version.

### Added
- Guided workflow (photos, control points, align, frame and crop, save) with an exposure fusion mode for bracketed shots.
- Automatic control points with a sky/cloud filter, a manual point editor (grow points from a few clicks, patch-click mode that uses the rotation and zoom of nearby points), patch-matching refinement, pair check and a connection graph.
- Memory-independent export: the picture is made strip by strip and streamed to JPEG, PNG, TIFF or BigTIFF; the result does not depend on the available memory. Free disk space is checked first. Target width in pixels, 16-bit TIFF, and cancelling without leaving a partial file.
- Multiband blending with a seam preference for the sharpest photo.
- Smart seams (on by default, can be switched off): a graph-cut seam finder that steers the seams around close objects such as poles and lamps, and that lets a zoomed-in photo win only where there is detail to gain (no coloured patches in the sky).
- Masks: positive/negative masks per photo (brush, lasso, eraser, undo/redo, "Show seams" view, right-click menu on photos) that steer the seams, saved in the project file.
- Smooth brightness correction across each photo (gain maps): evens out sky gradients, vignetting and automatic exposure between photos; option in the export dialog.
- Optional parallax correction near the seams, saved in the project file.
- RAW support as the optional Cargo feature `raw` (on by default; its crates are LGPL licensed). `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.md` lists all dependency licenses.
- Secondary windows (patch comparison, mask editor) are real OS windows.
- `--imgdiff` for comparing two pictures and experiment variables for the export and the seams (see the README).
- About 195 unit tests plus end-to-end tests with synthetic images; dual license `MIT OR Apache-2.0`; README screenshots.

### Changed
- `src/app.rs` split into `src/app/` modules (no behaviour change).
- The export always runs strip by strip; `PW_OLD_EXPORT=1` renders the whole picture in memory for comparing.
- Blending is wide ("Automatic") by default.

### Fixed
- With a parallax field, up to 25 visible rows at a photo's top or bottom edge could be cut off in the export.
